## Earlier planning reference


## Purpose

Provide an accessible, interactive introduction to musical understanding: how sounds and phrases are organized, how performance choices change what is heard, and how people can listen, respond, and create together.

**Working Persian title:** «شنیدن دیگری از طریق موسیقی»

## Audience

Persian-speaking listeners with mixed experience:
- curious beginners without instrumental training or music reading;
- people returning to music after earlier study;
- performing musicians and composers who want to examine musical relationships;
- participants interested in evidence-based discussion of listening.

No notation or instrumental skill is required. Offer deeper optional tasks to experienced participants.

## Current teaching method

Bernstein's *Young People's Concerts* provides the main instructional sequence. The facilitator:
1. Selects a short excerpt and prepares accurate Persian translation/subtitles.
2. Presents a concrete listening question before or during playback.
3. Stops at a purposeful moment to collect participants' observations.
4. Replays or deconstructs the demonstration to identify an audible change.
5. Uses familiar examples and a practical exercise.
6. Guides a participant response or original miniature creation.

**Repeated learning cycle:** شنیدن (listen) → تقلید کردن (imitate) → همراهی کردن (accompany/respond) → خلق کردن (create).

## Learning outcomes (proposed)

Participants will practice identifying phrases, repetition, contrast, tempo, articulation, timbre, motif development, and other relations; supporting interpretations with audible evidence; responding to another participant; and creating short musical ideas.

## Relationship to older material

The earlier *Hearing Each Other Through Music* four-session plan with Nina Simone, *Hair*, Persian wording, and critical-listening exercises is a **reference curriculum**, not automatically the definitive plan for this Bernstein-based course. Its examples may be adapted after review.

## Public collaboration context

A Persian proposal was prepared for **Iran Musicology**. It describes a multi-session, in-person or online format, with a suggested 75–90 minute meeting and 6–10 participants. These are **proposal figures**, not confirmed bookings or commitments.

## Scope and boundaries

- Emphasize musical listening and exploration over instrument technique.
- Encourage competing interpretations; distinguish sound, interpretation, and historical context.
- Avoid claiming every musical phrase communicates one definite story or emotion.
- Record exact references, timestamps, translation status, and third-party media rights.
- Keep announcements aligned with actual arrangements.

# Session 01 — Online Pilot: «آیا موسیقی داستانی را روایت می‌کند؟»

**Status:** PROPOSED, not yet rehearsed. **Video excerpt timecodes:** OPEN until verified on exact edition.

**Source:** Leonard Bernstein, *Young People's Concerts*, *What Does Music Mean?*.
Official source transcript: https://www.leonardbernstein.com/lectures/television-scripts/young-peoples-concerts/what-does-music-mean

## Intended learning outcome

After the activity, a beginner should be able to:
- describe at least one audible detail in a short musical passage;
- distinguish that observation from a personal story or emotional interpretation;
- compare two performances of a short phrase and identify a controlled change;
- offer a musical choice or response (by voice, chat or gesture).

**Pedagogical boundary:** Bernstein argues that musical meaning is not identical to an attached story. Present this as an argument for examining how sound works, not as a requirement to suppress subjective listening.

## A. First micro-pilot (25 minutes; 3–5 volunteers)

| Minute | Action | Facilitator's Persian wording / learning check |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Join and sound check, choice of chat or microphone | «اگر صدای موسیقی را واضح می‌شنوید، در چت یک علامت بگذارید.» |
| 3–7 | Play 15–25 s familiar instrumental passage, once | «چه احساس یا تصویری به ذهن‌تان آمد؟» |
| 7–10 | Listen again; demand audible evidence, not the 'correct' feeling | «فارغ از داستانی که تصور کردید، چه چیزی در خودِ صدا شنیدید؟» |
| 10–15 | Play one **verified, subtitled** Bernstein excerpt; pause before explanatory answer; discuss | «آیا توضیح برنشتاین چیزی به شنیدن ما اضافه می‌کند؟ چه چیزی را باید دوباره آزمایش کنیم؟» |
| 15–21 | Live experiment: play/sing original four-note or short original phrase in two ways; participants point to changed feature; select one new proposal | «کدام ویژگی تغییر کرد: سرعت، تکیه، کشش، یا پایان جمله؟ حالا شما یک تغییر پیشنهاد کنید.» |
| 21–25 | Play source again; each participant supplies one observation and one interpretation; brief exit check | «یک ویژگی را نام ببرید که واقعاً شنیدید، و بگویید چه برداشتی برای شما ایجاد کرد.» |

### What to observe

- Was there time for participants to answer **before** explanation?
- Were at least two participation routes available (chat + voice, and permission to pass)?
- Did audio compression erase the intended contrast?
- Did any participant name a distinct audible change and make a choice that the facilitator could reproduce?

## B. Candidate first full class (85 minutes; only after successful pilot)

| Time | Lesson block | Participant experience |
|---|---|---|
| 00–07 | Welcome, hearing/check-in and rules | Choose chat/voice participation |
| 07–17 | First familiar piece + two listens | Impression first; observation second |
| 17–29 | Bernstein excerpt and critical pause | Answer a question before explanation |
| 29–42 | Original facilitator melody demonstration | Muted imitation; volunteer comparison |
| 42–47 | Break | Rest |
| 47–60 | Phrase as musical question/answer | Listen for rising/ending movement; answer sequentially |
| 60–75 | Respond-and-create activity | A offers a short phrase, B responds after silence, group proposes deliberate variation |
| 75–85 | Return to original piece; assessment and close | Name one feature, give an interpretation with evidence, note next practice |

All clip counts and times are provisional; limit video so participants do more musical work than passive viewing.

## C. Audio and latency rules

- Demonstrate the exact same phrase with one changed variable at a time.
- Everyone can sing or clap *locally while muted*, but this cannot serve as proof of simultaneous group performance.
- Online group dialogue uses **sequential** cues: participant A completes; facilitator signals; B responds.
- If someone cannot sing, they can describe a variation and ask the facilitator to demonstrate it.
- Use shorter segments and the chat if connectivity degrades.
- Name a cohost to manage permissions and troubleshoot while the facilitator teaches.

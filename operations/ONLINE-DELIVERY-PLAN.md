## Two linked workstreams

1. **Learning design:** adapt short, verified Bernstein excerpts to concrete listening questions, Persian translation, familiar examples, accessible experiments, and assessment of what learners hear.
2. **Operations:** manage registration and joining, Google Meet audio delivery, moderation, clear communication, accessible participant roles and fallback when sound fails.

## Course scope

The **first four meetings** form the beginning of a continuing curriculum. The aim is to explore more Bernstein material progressively, but the entire series is *not* promised in four meetings. The revised Persian proposal sketches:

1. What do we hear, and what do we infer?
2. What does a small change in performance do?
3. How do we respond to someone else's sound or idea?
4. What can we discover by listening again and applying earlier questions to a fresh example?

These are working learning outcomes, not fully produced video lessons. Larger topics such as harmony, motivic development and form may be developed in later meetings.

### Important distinction: optional pilot vs. required checks

The current proposal explicitly treats a **volunteer pilot as optional**, not a prerequisite for existing registration. However, **the intended Google Meet audio, video/subtitle and fallback must be tested** before an actual class. Neither requirement should be presented as already completed.

## Roles (to be agreed)

- Joint: permissions, recording/consent policy, public messaging and ownership of assets.

## Live activity loop

**Hear** a short example → **understand** an observable feature → **discuss** interpretations with musical evidence → **experience** the feature through an original demonstration or participant choice → hear again.

For online musical response, use **sequential A finishes → B responds**. Muted local imitation is fine; do not promise synchronous remote ensemble playing on ordinary Meet calls. Provide chat and listening-only alternatives.

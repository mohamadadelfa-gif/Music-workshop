# Iran Musicology — Integrated Online Workshop Delivery Plan

**Version:** 0.1 (proposed) | **Prepared:** 2026-11-04 | **Status:** Planning; not an agreement or announced schedule.

## Project objective

Deliver an accessible, Persian-language online musical learning experience in which participants learn from selected, subtitled excerpts of Leonard Bernstein's *Young People's Concerts*, investigate what they hear, imitate and respond to musical ideas, and create short musical choices.

We must prove both:
1. **Pedagogical feasibility:** beginners can notice and explain audible changes, participate in a non-notation activity, and make a choice in response to another sound.
2. **Operational feasibility:** the intended participants can join, hear musical details, use chat/turn-taking, and continue after predictable technical failures.

**Canonical learning cycle:** شنیدن → تقلید کردن → همراهی کردن → خلق کردن.

## A. Scope and curriculum policy

The Iran Musicology proposal currently suggests four **themes**, not guaranteed coverage of every Bernstein episode:

1. Story, sound, and musical phrases; questions and answers.
2. Tempo, articulation, timbre, and changing expression.
3. Contrast, sonic dialogue, and words/emphasis.
4. Motif development, harmony, and short collaborative creation.

These are **proposed modules**, not approved lesson count or fixed syllabus. Bernstein's official website documents 53 *Young People's Concerts*. A comprehensive survey requires separately scoped phases; do not advertise all 53 in four meetings. See https://www.leonardbernstein.com/about/educator/young-peoples-concerts.

The earlier Nina Simone/*Hair* four-session workshop in the master document is valuable background, not automatically the approved sequence for the Iran Musicology partnership.

## D. Delivery schedule (relative to first class; proposed milestones)

- **T–14 days:** Agree on scope and owner; identify 1–2 candidate platforms; confirm how participants in Iran will connect.
- **T–10 days:** Select and verify one excerpt; write the 20–30 minute micro-pilot; prepare original live alternative.
- **T–7 days:** Run the platform/audio comparison with facilitator, cohost, and at least one representative participant connection.
- **T–5 days:** Run pilot with 3–5 volunteers, including a beginner; capture results.
- **T–3 days:** Apply one or two critical changes; finalize session material; obtain relevant media use clearance.
- **T–1 day:** Host/cohost rehearsal; invitation link and backup communication tested.
- **T–0:** Host joins early; deliver with cohost; collect a brief exit response.
- **T+1–2 days:** Record what worked, barriers, and a change for the next session.

These are planning checkpoints, not confirmed bookings.

## E. Session-level online teaching protocol

Repeat this loop for each short learning unit:

1. **Experience:** play a short, recognizable example; participants listen before theory.
2. **Individual first response:** chat, voice, gesture, or listening-only reflection.
3. **Evidence:** separate what is heard (pitch movement, rhythm, timbre, articulation) from an emotional or narrative interpretation.
4. **Bernstein excerpt:** pause at a productive question; replay/compare the original demonstration and discuss Bernstein's claim without turning it into an unquestionable rule.
5. **Experiment:** everyone imitates muted; one participant demonstrates; a second responds after the first stops, preserving one element and changing another.
6. **Return:** play the original again and ask what audible detail can now be identified.

### Latency rule

Ordinary conferencing is not a synchronized ensemble studio. Never make participants' success depend on all unmuting and playing a shared beat simultaneously. Use sequential call-and-response, locally muted practice, and optionally assembled contributions with prior consent. Specialized low-latency audio is optional and only after end-to-end testing.

### Participation and accessibility

- Always provide voice, chat, or gesture alternatives; passing is allowed.
- Persian written instructions are concise and readable on a phone.
- Explain English musical terminology **after** participants encounter the sound.
- Provide short pre-session joining instructions and a non-video fallback when bandwidth is poor.
- Do not publicly rank performers by pitch accuracy or prior schooling.

## F. Operational readiness gates

**No recruitment claims beyond the proposal until:** partner accepts course promise, dates, platform, fee/cancellation rules, and practical media use position.

**No full paid launch until:**
- [ ] beginner-facing outcome and first 20–30 minute experiment demonstrated;
- [ ] exact excerpt, subtitles, and speaker/source context checked;
- [ ] host and cohost successfully shared prerecorded music and live instrument sound with representative participant connections;
- [ ] one backup teaching path has been rehearsed;
- [ ] moderator, participant support and privacy/recording rules assigned;
- [ ] pilot evidence recorded and major confusion or audio failures addressed.
